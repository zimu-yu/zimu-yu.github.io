I am **Zimu Yu (余自牧)**, a research assistant working at the intersection of **machine learning and computational biology**. I am interested in how computational models can help us understand biological variation and guide the design of new protein variants.

My research centers on **protein fitness landscapes and mutational epistasis**: how individual mutations contribute to function, how their effects change in combination, and how we can predict combinations beyond those measured experimentally. I also work on representation learning for single-cell and multi-omics data.

I work with **Gong Lab at Tsinghua University**, **Xu Lab at Zhongnan University of Economics and Law**, and **Sun Lab at Emory University**. I received my bachelor's degree in Statistics from Zhongnan University of Economics and Law in 2025.

[View publications](/publications/) · [Explore research](/research/) · [Curriculum vitae](/cv/)

## Research interests

- **Protein fitness landscapes** — learning the relationship between sequence variation and protein function.
- **Mutational epistasis** — modeling interactions among mutations, including higher-order combinations.
- **Protein engineering** — using predictive models to prioritize variants for experimental study.
- **Single-cell omics** — learning representations of heterogeneous biological data.

## Selected research

### Cerebra-Epistasis
A reusable mutation atlas for efficient prediction of multi-mutant fitness and epistasis, connecting single-sequence structural representations with mutation-level components.

[Project overview](/research/#cerebra-epistasis) · [Code](https://github.com/Gonglab-THU/Cerebra-Epistasis)

### MetaAI
Learning sequence–metabolite landscapes from sparse experimental measurements to support epistasis prediction and protein-variant design.

[Project overview](/research/#metaai) · [Code](https://github.com/xulab-research/MetaAI-research)

### RADAR & CAPE
Representation learning for single-cell omics and non-sequential features, spanning anomalous-cell discovery and causality-aware positional encoding.

[Project overviews](/research/#radar) · [Publications](/publications/)
