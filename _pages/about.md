Hi, welcome to my page!

I'm Zimu Yu. I studied Statistics at Zhongnan University of Economics and Law (ZUEL). My interest in research began with mathematics, particularly probability theory and mathematical statistics. When faced with a complex question, I enjoy reasoning through it step by step, breaking it into smaller pieces, and using mathematical and statistical thinking to understand how they fit together.

I did not start out knowing that I would work in computational biology. My interest in the field developed gradually through research, as I became increasingly interested in biological questions that could be studied with statistical and computational methods. I currently work as a research assistant with [Prof. Haipeng Gong's group at Tsinghua University](https://life.tsinghua.edu.cn/lifeen/info/1034/1086.htm), [Prof. Yunxin Xu's group at ZUEL](https://tsxy.zuel.edu.cn/2025/0527/c14199a392774/page.htm), and [Prof. Xiaobo Sun's group at Emory University](https://med.emory.edu/directory/profile/?u=XSUN28).

Most of my recent work has focused on protein fitness landscapes, mutation effect prediction, mutational epistasis, and single-cell omics. These projects have made me increasingly interested in using statistics and machine learning to study biological systems. I am especially interested in questions about how mutations affect protein function and how cellular heterogeneity can be studied using single-cell and multi-omics data.

I hope to pursue a Ph.D. and continue working on problems at the intersection of statistics, machine learning, and computational biology. I enjoy using mathematical, statistical, and computational tools to tackle challenging problems in biology.

I am always happy to talk about related research. Please feel free to reach out by email.

## Research interests

I am particularly interested in protein fitness landscapes, mutational epistasis, protein variant effect prediction, and protein engineering. More broadly, I am interested in statistical machine learning and AI for biological and scientific problems.
